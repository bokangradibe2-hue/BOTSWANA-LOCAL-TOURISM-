Are you ready for an adventure of a lifetime in the heart of Africa? Botswana is a premier safari destination that will leave you awestruck. In this guide, we’ll take you through the best places to visit in Botswana for a memorable vacation.

National Parks & Wildlife Reserves | Botswana Places to Visit

Botswana is known for its national parks and wildlife reserves, which cover almost 40% of the country. These protected areas are a haven for wildlife enthusiasts, nature lovers, and adventure seekers. Let’s explore the top national parks and wildlife reserves you shouldn’t miss!

Okavango Delta

Imagine gliding through the tranquil waters of the Okavango Delta, surrounded by greenery and wildlife. This UNESCO World Heritage site is the world’s largest inland delta, covering over 15,000 sq km.

Explore the delta’s waterways in a traditional dugout canoe known as a mokoro. Spot over 400 bird species and mammals, including elephants, lions, and leopards. For the best wildlife viewing, visit between May and September. That’s the dry season when animals congregate around water sources.

Chobe National Park

Imagine witnessing one of Africa’s largest concentrations of game, with an astonishing 120,000 elephants calling Chobe National Park home. This park, covering 11,700 sq km, is a wildlife sanctuary and one of Botswana’s best places to visit.

Take a scenic cruise on the Chobe River, go on a thrilling game drive, or try fishing. Visit between May and October during the dry season, when the abundance of wildlife will mesmerize you. In October, the water-based wildlife viewing peaks, with animals gathering around the river.

Moremi Game Reserve

Imagine exploring the landscapes and ecosystems of the Okavango Delta, with its network of waterways, lagoons, and lush vegetation. The Moremi Game Reserve, covering roughly 40% of the delta, is a refuge for nature lovers and wildlife enthusiasts.

Experience game drives, boat safaris, and birdwatching to spot African wild dogs, cheetahs, leopards, and birdlife. Visit between July and October, in the dry season, for the best wildlife viewing around water sources.

Central Kalahari Game Reserve

Are you ready for a unique desert safari experience? The Central Kalahari Game Reserve, the world’s second-largest wildlife reserve, is a must-visit destination. This vast reserve spans approximately 52,800 sq km.

Join a guided game drive to spot black-maned Kalahari lions, cheetahs, giraffes, and meerkats. Visit the San Bushmen communities to learn about their traditions and way of life. Indulge in stargazing as the sun sets, surrounded by the desert landscape.

For the best experience, visit between December and March during the green season. During this time, temporary water sources attract animals, and the desert landscape becomes a vibrant oasis.

Makgadikgadi Pans National Park

Imagine standing on one of the world’s largest salt flats, surrounded by a surreal landscape of white salt and silence. The Makgadikgadi Pans National Park is a unique gem in Botswana, featuring an otherworldly landscape and archaeological treasures. Explore the pans on a quad bike and visit meerkat colonies. Interact with the San people to learn about their ancient traditions.

In the wet season (November to March), witness zebra and wildebeest migrations and spot flamingos and pelicans. In the dry season (April to October), focus on exploring landscapes and cultural experiences.

Tsodilo Hills

Explore the “Louvre of the Desert” and uncover human history at the Tsodilo Hills, a UNESCO World Heritage site. This sacred place has over 4,500 ancient rock paintings, offering insight into the lives of the San people and their ancestors. Join a guided walk to discover the intricacies of these artworks and delve into the area’s archaeology and mythology.

Tsodilo Hills, with its rich cultural significance, is a must-visit destination in Botswana. The cooler months (May to August) are more comfortable for hiking and exploring.

City Breaks | Botswana Places to Visit

Botswana’s cities blend tradition and modernity, contrasting with its natural landscapes. Explore the streets, museums, and local cuisine to experience the country’s urban culture.

Top Cities to Visit

  • Gaborone: Botswana’s capital is known for modern architecture and bustling streets. Visit the National Museum and Art Gallery to learn about the country’s history and culture. Explore the Gaborone Game Reserve, a tranquil oasis in the city’s heart. Enjoy traditional cuisine at local restaurants.
  • Maun: The gateway to the Okavango Delta, this place is a hub for adventure seekers. Take a scenic flight for breathtaking views. Visit the Nhabe Museum to learn about the region’s history and wildlife. Stock up on supplies at outdoor shops.
  • Kasane: Located near Chobe National Park, this place is a haven for wildlife enthusiasts. Take a river cruise or game drive to spot elephants, hippos, and crocodiles. Visit the Kazungula Ferry, a vital crossing point over the Zambezi River. Enjoy a sundowner cruise on the Chobe River for stunning views of the African sunset.

Natural Wonders | Botswana Places to Visit

Botswana has breathtaking natural wonders, from the mighty Victoria Falls to the unique landscapes of Nxai Pan National Park.

Top Natural Wonders

  • Victoria Falls: This attraction is one of the Seven Natural Wonders of the World, shared with Zambia and Zimbabwe. Take a helicopter tour or whitewater rafting. Explore the Victoria Falls National Park and spot wildlife. Marvel at the sheer power and beauty of the falls from various viewpoints.
  • Nxai Pan National Park: This park is famous for its ancient baobab trees, estimated to be over 1,000 years old. Take a guided walk to learn about the ecosystem and wildlife. Spot animals like elephants, lions, and cheetahs. Enjoy a scenic drive through the park’s landscapes.
  • Kubu Island: This rocky outcrop in the Makgadikgadi Pans is known for its breathtaking sunsets. On a guided tour, explore the unique geological formations. Spot birdlife, including flamingos and pelicans. Enjoy a picnic surrounded by the surreal landscape of the Makgadikgadi Pans.

Cultural Experiences | Botswana Places to Visit

Botswana has a rich cultural heritage, with diverse communities that offer a unique glimpse into the country’s traditions. As you explore the natural wonders, immerse yourself in the local culture and experience the people’s hospitality.

Botswana’s Cultures and Traditions

Botswana has diverse ethnic groups, such as the Tswana, Kalanga, Basarwa, and Kgalagadi. Each has a distinct language, customs, and traditions shaped by history, environment, and cultural heritage. From the San Bushmen’s hunter-gatherer traditions to the Tswana’s cattle-herding heritage, every group has a unique story.

Top Cultural Experiences

  • Visit Traditional Villages: Experience the daily life of Botswana’s indigenous folks. Learn about the San Bushmen’s ancient hunting and gathering techniques, crafts, and artwork. Alternatively, visit a Tswana village to discover the importance of cattle in their culture, music, and dance traditions.
  • Attend Traditional Dance or Music Performance: Watch dancers in vibrant costumes perform energetic routines accompanied by traditional instruments.
  • Take a Cooking Class: Learn the secrets of Botswana’s unique cuisine, combining African, European, and Asian flavors. Discover how to prepare staple dishes like seswaa (stewed meat), tsamma melon soup, and makati (fried dough cakes).

FAQ | Botswana Places to Visit

Q1: What is the best time to visit Botswana for wildlife viewing?

The best time for wildlife viewing in Botswana is during the dry season, from May to October. During this time, animals gather around water sources, making them easier to spot. The peak months are June to August, with temperatures ranging from 6°C at night to 25°C during the day.

Q2: What is the average cost of a safari in Botswana?

The safari cost in Botswana varies widely. During peak season, luxury lodges cost up to $2,000 per person per night. Budget options, like self-drive safaris, can reduce costs, with vehicle rentals averaging $150 per day.

Q3: Are there direct international flights to Botswana?

Botswana’s main international gateway is Sir Seretse Khama International Airport in Gaborone. However, most international tourists arrive via Johannesburg (South Africa) with a short connecting flight. Direct flights from major cities are limited.

Q4: What languages do people in Botswana speak?

English is widely spoken, especially in tourist areas and among younger generations. Setswana is the national language and is spoken by the majority.

Q5: Is Botswana safe for tourists?

Botswana is one of the safest African countries for tourists. Common sense precautions are advised, especially in urban areas. Political stability and a low crime rate contribute to tourist safety.

Q6: Are there family-friendly places to visit in Botswana?

Botswana has family-friendly destinations like Makgadikgadi Pans, where kids can enjoy safaris and interactive game drives. The Khama Rhino Sanctuary has a petting zoo and animal encounters.

Get Ready for Stunning Botswana Experiences

Botswana offers remarkable experiences for travelers, from wildlife safaris to cultural encounters. Stay flexible, open to new experiences, and respect the local environment and culture as you plan your trip.

A traveler from Milwaukee, Wisconsin, who recently visited Botswana had this to say:

“The beauty and wildlife of Botswana blew me away. The Okavango Delta and Chobe National Park were truly memorable experiences for me and my family. We can’t wait to go back!”
