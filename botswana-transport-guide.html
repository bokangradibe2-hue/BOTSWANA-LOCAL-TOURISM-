TL;DR: How to Get Around Botswana Efficiently

Getting around Botswana is straightforward if you mix airport transfers, licensed taxis, inter-city buses, and self-drive for national parks, while planning around limited public transport. For most travellers, pre-booked airport pickups, reliable bus companies, and a rental 4x4 for safaris offer the best balance of cost, comfort, and flexibility.

Botswana has solid roads between major towns, basic but usable public transport, and very limited formal ride-hailing. Gaborone and Maun are your main gateways, with taxis and pre-arranged transfers the norm from the airports. Inter-city buses connect Gaborone, Maun, Francistown, Kasane, and border posts at affordable prices, though services can be slow and not always punctual.

There’s no metro system and only limited rail for passengers, so buses and cars do most of the heavy lifting. Many visitors rent vehicles—often 4x4s—for trips into places like the Okavango Delta, Moremi, and Chobe. Botswana Airport Transfers: Gaborone, Maun, and Kasane

Airport transfers in Botswana are mostly via pre-booked shuttles and metered or negotiated taxis, with typical one-way costs from US$10–50 in 2026 depending on distance and comfort. For most travellers, arranging a transfer through your lodge or tour operator is the smoothest way to start your trip.

Sir Seretse Khama International Airport (Gaborone) is about 15–20 minutes from the city centre. In 2026, expect:

  • City taxi to central Gaborone: 120–200 BWP (≈ US$9–15). Agree the fare before setting off.
  • Hotel-arranged transfer: 200–350 BWP (≈ US$15–26), often bundled into room rates for business hotels.

Maun Airport, the main gateway for the Okavango Delta, sits just a few kilometres from town. Because so many visitors connect to lodges and scenic flights:

  • Lodge/agency transfers are frequently included or cost 100–250 BWP (≈ US$8–19) each way.
  • Local taxis for short hops around Maun are usually 30–60 BWP (≈ US$2–4.50).

Kasane Airport serves Chobe National Park and the Zambezi region. Many lodges near the Chobe River include airport pickup; if not, budget 150–250 BWP (≈ US$11–19) for a private transfer.

Botswana received over 1.8 million international arrivals in 2023 (per Botswana Tourism Organisation), many bound for safaris, so airports can be busy in peak season (June–October). Botswana Public Transport Guide: Buses, Minibuses, and Trains

Botswana public transport relies on buses and shared minibuses rather than metro or extensive passenger rail, with fares between major towns typically 80–200 BWP (≈ US$6–15) in 2026. It’s cheap and authentic, but expect basic comfort, limited schedules, and cash-only payments.

There’s no metro system and only limited passenger rail. Botswana Railways runs occasional services, but most visitors and locals use road transport. From Gaborone, Maun, Francistown, and Kasane you’ll find:

  • Inter-city buses: Larger coaches on main routes (e.g., Gaborone–Francistown, Francistown–Maun) with fixed stops.
  • Minibuses/“combis”: 15-seater vans connecting suburbs and smaller towns; they depart when full.

Typical 2026 fares:

  • Gaborone–Francistown (≈ 450 km): 120–170 BWP (US$9–13) by coach.
  • Francistown–Maun (≈ 500 km): 150–200 BWP (US$11–15).
  • In-city combis: 6–10 BWP (under US$1) per ride.

Public transport is cash-based; there are no universal transport cards or passes. Carry small bills and coins (10–50 BWP notes) and keep valuables close. According to the World Bank, over 70% of Botswana’s population lives in urban areas, and services are concentrated along the A1 corridor, so rural and park areas have very limited public transport.

Ride-Hailing, Taxis, and Local Transport Apps in Botswana

Botswana’s ride-hailing scene is still emerging, so travellers should rely mainly on licensed taxis, hotel shuttles, and pre-arranged drivers rather than expecting Uber-style coverage. Always confirm the fare upfront, carry cash, and keep your accommodation’s phone number handy.

International platforms like Uber, Bolt, and Grab are not widely available in Botswana as of 2026. In Gaborone and sometimes Francistown, a few local apps and Facebook or WhatsApp-based services operate, but coverage can be patchy. The most reliable options are:

  • Hotel or guesthouse taxis: Often trusted drivers with set rates.
  • Street taxis: Marked sedans operating around bus ranks and shopping centres.
  • Private transfers: Arranged through safari operators or travel agencies, especially in Maun and Kasane.

Typical 2026 taxi pricing:

  • Short city rides in Gaborone: 40–80 BWP (≈ US$3–6).
  • Airport–city centre: 120–200 BWP (≈ US$9–15) as noted earlier.

Taxis are usually shared only if you agree; otherwise, ask for a “special” (private) ride. Fares are negotiated, not metered, so it’s helpful to ask your hotel for ballpark prices beforehand.

Inter-City Travel in Botswana: Buses, Flights, and Tour Transfers

For getting between Botswana’s major hubs, long-distance buses, domestic flights, and lodge transfers form the core options, with costs from around US$10 by bus to US$150+ by air in 2026. The right choice depends on your time, budget, and comfort priorities.

Long-distance buses are the most budget-friendly way to travel between cities:

  • Gaborone–Maun: 180–250 BWP (≈ US$14–19), 9–10 hours.
  • Gaborone–Kasane (via Nata): 250–350 BWP (≈ US$19–26), often 11+ hours with changes.
  • Francistown–Kasane: 180–250 BWP (≈ US$14–19), around 7–8 hours.

Book at bus ranks or via local agencies; there’s rarely online booking. Buses can be crowded, and air-con may be minimal—travel early in the morning where possible.

Domestic flights with regional carriers link Gaborone, Maun, Francistown, and Kasane, as well as airstrips in the Okavango and Chobe area:

  • Gaborone–Maun: from about 2,000–3,000 BWP (≈ US$150–230) one way in 2026.
  • Maun–Kasane: 2,500–3,500 BWP (≈ US$190–270) on light aircraft.

They’re pricey but save long road hours, especially on short itineraries. According to Botswana’s Ministry of Transport and Public Works, road transport still carries the majority of domestic passengers, but demand for regional flights has been steadily growing with safari tourism.

Many travellers also rely on lodge and tour transfers—for example, Maun–Moremi or Kasane–Chobe Riverfront—often bundled into package prices. Confirm whether road or air transfers are included when comparing safari quotes, as this can add hundreds of dollars to your budget.

Driving in Botswana: Car Rental, 4x4 Safaris, and Road Safety

Self-driving in Botswana is an excellent way to reach national parks and remote lodges, especially with a 4x4, but requires solid preparation, careful planning, and respect for wildlife and road conditions. Travellers should check insurance details, park rules, and seasonal access before hitting the road.

Car rental & costs (2026 estimates):

  • Standard sedan (good for highways): from 600–900 BWP/day (≈ US$45–70).
  • 4x4 with high clearance: 1,200–2,000 BWP/day (≈ US$90–150).
  • Fully equipped 4x4 with rooftop tent: 1,800–2,800 BWP/day (≈ US$135–210).

Fuel prices fluctuate, but budget around 14–18 BWP per litre (≈ US$1–1.30) in 2026. Botswana drives on the left-hand side, and major roads like the A1 are generally paved and in decent condition.

Most visitors can drive with a valid national licence in English, but an International Driving Permit (IDP) is recommended and sometimes required by rental agencies. Check age limits (often 23–25 minimum) and credit card deposit requirements.

Key safety tips:

  • Avoid night driving due to livestock and wildlife on the road.
  • Keep fuel topped up; distances can be long between stations.
  • In parks like Moremi and Chobe, carry recovery gear and a paper map; tracks can flood in the wet season.

Common Questions About Getting Around Botswana

Most travellers get around Botswana using a mix of airport transfers, long-distance buses, and rental cars or tour vehicles, with taxis filling in the gaps in towns. Planning key routes in advance—and knowing when public transport isn’t practical—will save you time, money, and stress.

Is there a metro or extensive train network in Botswana?
No. There’s no metro and only limited passenger rail. Almost all tourist movement is by road or air, using buses, minibuses, private shuttles, and domestic flights.

Can I rely only on public transport for a safari trip?
Not realistically. While you can reach hubs like Maun or Kasane by bus, national parks and remote lodges typically require 4x4 vehicles, charter flights, or lodge transfers. Most safari travellers book packages that include transport.

Do I need an International Driving Permit?
If your licence is in English and includes your photo, you can often use it, but many rental companies recommend or require an IDP. It’s smart to carry one to avoid issues at checkpoints or with insurers.

Are taxis safe and how do I pay?
Licensed taxis from reputable operators are generally safe. Pay in cash (Botswana pula) and agree the fare beforehand. Keep small notes to avoid change issues.

How much should I budget for daily transport?
For a mid-range traveller outside of safari packages, 150–400 BWP/day (≈ US$11–30) is typical, covering a couple of taxi rides and occasional inter-city buses. Safari days with charter flights or long 4x4 transfers will cost significantly more.
